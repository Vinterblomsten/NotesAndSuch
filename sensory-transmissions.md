
**Recepter cells** are biological **transducers** that can convert energy from different types of physical energy frem sensory stimuli, to electrical energy of membrane- and actionpotential.

#### Categorization of sensory cells
After **origin of stimuli**
- **Exteroceptors** - Register stimuli on the organism surface (Touch, taste, heat, etc.)
- **Interoception** - Registor internal stimuli (Blood pressure, $O_2$ and $CO_2$ )
- **Proprioception** - The position and movement of body parts (Motor system and efferent copies)

After the **adequte stimuli** for the **sensory receptors**
- **Photoreceptors** - reacts to lights (Sight)
- **Kemoreceptors** - reacts to chemicals (Taste, smells)
- **Mechanocepters** - reacts to mechanical deformation (Touch, sound)
- **Termoceptors** - reacts to temperature
- **Nociceptors** - reacts to tissue damage and trauma (Pain)

#### Dynamic area og senses
The afferent axon can transmit signals between 3 Hz and 300 Hz
<img src="./z-Figures/Screenshot 2026-09-21 at 08.39.24.png" alt="Screenshot 2026-09-21 at 08.39.24" width="220" />

The **dynamic area** is the stimulus-strength interval, where to different stimulus strength will result in two different impulse frequencies. If this interval was two large, we would not be able to differentiate between small stimulis strength differences. Instead the area is **dynamic**, meaning that at can be small enough for us to differentiate small changes (when in the right area), while being moveable to fit the **average stimuli** at a given moment - adaptation. 

**$K_A$ channels** in the transmitter part decreases the frequency of APs, making the registration og larger sensory stimuli possible - They define the width of the interval. 
- The $K_A$ channels are **closed** and **inactivated** at the resting potential
- When the cell as hyperpolarized, they **open**
- When the cell depolarizes (New AP) they are **inactivated**

##### Tonic receptors
The **tonic** receptors adapts **slowly** to change the dynamic area to the **average** stimulis strength
- This however means, that some stimuli are outside of the dynamic areas, and you are "blind" until adaptation
<img src="./z-Figures/Screenshot 2026-09-21 at 08.50.39.png" alt="Screenshot 2026-09-21 at 08.50.39" width="220" />
The tonic receptors are **slowly adapting**, meaning, that they register the stimulus strength (or rather the **stimulus change $\Delta S$**, as the adaptation of the dynamic areas, means that the absolut stimuli strength can't be know)
- The dynamic area doesn't shift by small variations in the stimuli close to the average
- It does however change, when the average shifts (E.g. stepping into a dark room, from the outside)
	- This when "understimulating" we experience an interval with no activity before the adaptation catches up - the **Post Excitatory Pause (PEP)**

##### Phasic receptors
The **phasic** receptors detect the temporal dynamics of stimuli change
<img src="./z-Figures/Screenshot 2026-09-21 at 08.54.15.png" alt="Screenshot 2026-09-21 at 08.54.15" width="220" />
The **dynamic area** adapts almost as fast as the stimuli, meaning that the frequency of the APs reflect how fast the change og stimuli strength happens, that is they detect $\Delta S/\Delta t$ 
- This means that phasic receptors don't registrate constant stimuli, as $\Delta S/\Delta t=0$

Many receptors both somewhat phasic and somewhat tonic, meaning that they are sensitive to $\Delta S$ and $\Delta S/\Delta t$ - these are called **tonic-phasic receptors**

##### Cause of Adaptation
The adaptation **happens as a part** of every proces - **Absorption**, **Transduction**, **Transformation** and **outside of the receptor**

**Outside** of the receptor (**Before** the receptor)
- Anatomical changes - e.g. pupils regulates the light on the retina
- The **changes the receptor potential**

During the **absorption**/**transduction**
- **Deregulation** of the receptor (e.g. rhodopsin) or the **transduction mechanism** as a result of stimulation
- This **changes the receptor potential**

During the **transformation**
- The depolarization opens the $Na^+$ channels and depolarizes the cell
- Voltage gated $Ca^{2+}$ channels opens at each AP
	- The $Ca^{2+}$ is removed gradually, but not fast enough
- $K_C$ channels open proporitonal to the amount of $Ca^{2+}$
	- These prolong the refractory period and the frequence of APs decrease
- This continues until the $Ca^{2+}$ is in equilibrium (As the frequency of APs increase, less $Ca^{2+}$ enters the cell, while the efflux is constant)
- This does **not change the receptor potential**
