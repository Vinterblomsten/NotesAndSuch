#ExperimentalMethods 
In general, neural methods are split into to sorts of categories. In both of these categories there exists both invasive and noninvasive methods. 
1. **Brain perturbation approach** (**Somatic Intervention**): where there occurs an perturbation of the brain (naturally or as a part of the experiment), and we then do measurements on task performances. ([[Studying the Damaged Brain|Lesion-testing]], [[Methods to Pertub  Neural Function#Transcranial Magnetic Stimulation (TMS)|TMS]], [[Methods to Pertub  Neural Function#Transcranial direct current stimulation (tDCS)|tDCS]], [[Methods to Pertub  Neural Function#Transcranial alternating current stimulation (tACS)|tACS]], [[Methods to Pertub  Neural Function#Optogenetics|optogenetics]])
2. **Neuromonitoring approach (Behavioral intervention)**: where we do manipulation of cognitive processes in different ways, and then we measure the neural variables, that is we do different kinds of imaging or measurements of brain areas.  ([[Methods to Meassure Neural Activity#Single-Cell Neurophysiology|single neuron measurements]], [[Methods to Meassure Neural Activity#Electroencephalography (EEG)|EEG]], [[Methods to Meassure Neural Activity#Even-Related Potentials (ERP)|ERP]], [[Methods to Meassure Neural Activity#Magnetoencephalography (MEG)|MEG]], [[Neuroimaging - MRI and PET#Positron Emission Tomography (PET)|PET]], [[Neuroimaging - MRI and PET#Functional Magnetic Resonance Imaging|fMRI]], [[Structural Analysis of the Brain#Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI or MR)|MR(I)]], [[Methods to Meassure Neural Activity#Functional Near Infrared Optical Imaging Systems (fNIR)|fNIRS]], eye-tracking)
