##### Pharmacology
The release of [[Synapses#Neurotransmitters|neurotransmitters]] at neural [[Synapses]] and the resultants are critical fir information transfer from one neuron to the next. Drugs known as psychoactive (eg. caffeine, alcohol, and cocaine, as well as neuropharmaceutical drugs) can cross the [[Cells and tissue of the Nervous System#Blood-brain barrier|blood-brain barrier (BBB)]] which often result in changes in the cognitive function. **Pharmacological studies** are often categorized in two groups: 
**Agonist drugs:** These have a chemical structure similar to certain [[Synapses#Neurotransmitters|neurotransmitters]], mimicking their actions allowing for researchers to test the effects of certain transmitters in tasks.
**Antagonist drugs:** These have a chemical structure, that blocks the neurotransmitters, dampening their effects. This allows researches to investigate the role of certain transmitters, or reduce the activity of certain areas.
A drawback of using blood injected to the blood stream, is the lack of specificity, making for a uncertainty of the drugs effect on other areas, and the amount of the drug reaching the desired area.

##### Genetic Manipulation
Genetic manipulations have played a major role in neuroscience studies using animal models. **Knockout technology** enables scientists to explore the consequences of the lack of expression of a specific gene in order to determine its role in behavior. [[#Optogenetics]] provide the experimenter with the ability to control neuronal activity in targeted cells.

##### Invasive Stimulation Methods
In almost every case we will avoid invasive procedures for experimenting on humans, but it is quite useful with animal testing (when done ethically - Find literature?) and on patients with neurological problems, that require surgical intervention. 

###### Deep Brain Stimulation (DBS)
DBS is a procedure, where an electrode is surgically implanted in specific brain regions for an extended period to modulate neuronal activity. Clinically this is used especially for treating parkinsons disease (An electrode in the subcortex, usually the subthalamic nucleus og the basal ganglia).

This is also used "live" during clinical surgery, where you do electrical stimulation and measureing behavioural response in order to figure out the functionings of specific areas
- For example in order to know how much of the area around a tumor that are nonessential that can be removed


###### Optogenetics
This is a method of using lights to control the activation of a specific region of neurons, by using **viral transduction** to start the production of light-sensitive ion channels and thereby converting the neurons into "artificial photorecepters", which in turn can be "turned on"/activated with a tiny implanted optical fiber. (Alternatively **opsins** (light-sensitive ion channels) sensitive to light at infrared wave-length is advantageous, as it allows for light that can penetrate the tissue, and thereby eliminating the need for a surgically implanted optical fiber).

##### Noninvasive Stimulation Methods
The long range of noninvasive methods for modulating neural function are used both in a research capacity, a clinical capacity and with increasingly popularity as a tool for therapeutic purposes. 

These methods are often better then when using neuropsychological studies, with patients and control groups, at testing specific functional hypothesises as they can serve as their own control because the effects of these stimulations procedures are transient (temporary).

###### Transcranial Magnetic Stimulation (TMS)
The TMS device consists of a tightly wrapped wire coil encased in an insulated sheath and connected to powerful electrical capacitors. Triggering the capacitors sends a large electrical current through the coil and, as with all electrical currents, generates a magnetic field. The magnetic field propagates through the layers of the scalp and skull, reaching the cortical surface below the coil and altering the electrical activity in the neurons; with a modest level of stimulation, the neurons briefly fire.

![[Screenshot 2025-02-06 at 21.15.27.png|500]]

Researchers can administer TMS pulses at various intensities, timings (single, repetitive, continuous), and frequencies. They can do this “online” (while the participant performs a task) or “offline” (using protocols in which the consequences of TMS are known to last well beyond the duration of the stimulation itself).

 In **repetitive TMS** (rTMS), the researcher applies bursts of TMS pulses over several minutes. At a low frequency (1 cycle per second, or 1 Hz) applied over 10 to 15 minutes, this procedure has the eff ect of depressing cortical excitability . At higher frequencies, such as 10 Hz, excitability increases.
 
TMS has become a valuable research tool in cognitive neuroscience because of its ability to induce “virtual lesions”. However, as with all methods, TMS has its limitations. With currently available coils, the area of primary activation has about a 1-cm radius and thus can activate only relatively superficial areas.

###### Transcranial direct current stimulation (tDCS)
In tDCS, a current is sent between two electrodes on the scalp: an anode and a cathode. Physiological studies show that neurons under the anode become depolarized; that is, they achieve an elevated state of excitability, close to threshold, making them more likely to initiate an action potential when a stimulus or movement occurs (see Chapter 2). Neurons under the cathode become hyperpolarized: Pushed farther away from threshold, they become less likely to fire. 

On the other hand, it alter activity over a larger area, resulting in poor spatial resolution. It also can cause temporary change to  wide range of sensory, motor and cognitive tasks. 

Usually Anodal tDCS leads to improvements in performance (more excited neurons), but cathodal stimulation doesn't consistently impair performance.

###### Transcranial alternating current stimulation (tACS)
Rather than remaining constant as in tDCS, the electrical current oscillates in tACS allowing researchers to modulate the oscillation as another variable to intervent with brain function. Research has shown, that different frequencies of brain oscillations are associated with different cognitive functions, so perhaps this can be differentiated with tACS.

###### Transcranial Static Magnetic Stimulation (tSMS)
The newer and less expensive method, tSMS, uses strong magnets to create magnetic fields that, as with TMS, perturb electrical activity and thus temporarily alter cortical (outer layer of cerebrum) function. 

###### Transcranial Focused Ultrasound (tFUS)
This involves the generation of a low-intensity , low-frequency ultrasound signal. Th is signal increases the activity of voltage- gated sodium and calcium channels, thus triggering action potentials. Early work with this method has shown that tFUS can produce focal effects limited to a 5-mm area.
