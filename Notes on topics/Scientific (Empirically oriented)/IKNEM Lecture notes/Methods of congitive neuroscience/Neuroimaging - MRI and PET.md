##### Positron Emission Tomography (PET)
Positron Emission Tomography (PET) is a functional neuroimaging technique that measures brain activity by detecting the distribution of a radioactive tracer injected into the bloodstream. The tracer, often a form of glucose or another biologically active molecule, accumulates in regions of the brain with high metabolic activity, as these areas consume more energy. As the tracer decays, it emits positrons that collide with electrons, producing gamma rays detected by the PET scanner. This allows researchers to map metabolic activity and identify areas of the brain that are active during specific tasks or at rest. PET provides valuable insights into brain function and is particularly useful for studying neurotransmitter systems, blood flow, and metabolic changes in neurological and psychiatric disorders. However, it has lower spatial and temporal resolution compared to fMRI and involves exposure to ionizing radiation.

##### Functional Magnetic Resonance Imaging (fMRI)
Functional Magnetic Resonance Imaging (fMRI) is a powerful neuroimaging technique used to study brain activity by measuring changes in blood flow and oxygenation. It works by detecting the **Blood Oxygen Level-Dependent (BOLD)** signal, which arises from the brain's hemodynamic response to neural activity. fMRI provides high spatial resolution, allowing researchers to map brain function and identify active regions during specific tasks or at rest. Unlike techniques that directly measure electrical activity, fMRI offers an indirect but non-invasive way to observe brain function, making it a cornerstone of cognitive neuroscience, clinical research, and studies of brain connectivity. However, its temporal resolution is limited due to the slow nature of the BOLD response.

###### Block Design versus Event-Related Design
In fMRI experiments, **block design** and **event-related design** are two common approaches to organizing stimuli. 

- **Block Design:** Stimuli of the same type are grouped into blocks, with each block lasting several seconds. This design maximizes the BOLD signal strength and is ideal for detecting sustained brain activity in specific regions. However, it lacks precise temporal resolution and cannot separate individual stimulus responses.

- **Event-Related Design:** Stimuli are presented as individual events, either randomly or systematically spaced in time. This design allows for the analysis of brain responses to single events and provides better temporal resolution. It is more flexible for studying rapid cognitive processes but may yield weaker BOLD signals compared to block designs.
 
The choice depends on the research question: block design for strong, sustained activity patterns, and event-related design for detailed temporal analysis of individual stimuli.

##### PET or fMRI:

| **Feature**              | **PET (Positron Emission Tomography)**                                                                                                                                        | **fMRI (functional Magnetic Resonance Imaging)**                                                                                |
| ------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **What It Measures**     | Metabolic activity, neurotransmitter systems, receptor binding using radioactive tracers.                                                                                     | Blood Oxygen Level-Dependent (BOLD) signal reflecting neural activity indirectly.                                               |
| **Spatial Resolution**   | Lower (typically 4-6 mm).                                                                                                                                                     | Higher (typically 1-3 mm).                                                                                                      |
| **Temporal Resolution**  | Lower (minutes).                                                                                                                                                              | Moderate (seconds, limited by hemodynamic response).                                                                            |
| **Radiation Exposure**   | Yes (uses radioactive tracers).                                                                                                                                               | No (non-invasive, no radiation).                                                                                                |
| **Strengths**            | - Tracks molecular and metabolic processes.<br>- Studies neurotransmitter systems and receptor binding.<br>- Useful for clinical conditions like Alzheimer’s and Parkinson’s. | - High spatial resolution.<br>- Non-invasive and safe for repeated use.<br>- Ideal for mapping brain activity and connectivity. |
| **Limitations**          | - Involves radiation exposure.<br>- Lower spatial and temporal resolution.<br>- Expensive and less widely available.                                                          | - Indirect measure of neural activity.<br>- Limited temporal resolution.<br>- Cannot measure molecular processes directly.      |
| **Primary Applications** | - Metabolic and molecular studies.<br>- Neurotransmitter and receptor imaging.<br>- Clinical diagnostics (e.g., dementia, tumors).                                            | - Cognitive neuroscience.<br>- Brain mapping and connectivity studies.<br>- Task-based and resting-state research.              |
