##### Causes of Neurological Dysfunction

###### **Vascular disorders**
The brain use 20% of the oxygen we breathe, although it makes up for 2% of our mass, and it needs a constant flow in order to function. As little as 10 minutes of oxygen deprivation can cause neuronal death. This occurs in **Cerebral vascular accidents**, or strokes, where the blood flow is disrupted. ![[Screenshot 2025-02-02 at 16.43.28.png]]
Other types of cerebral [[#Vascular disorders]] can lead to ischemia (inadequate blood supply): A drop in blood pressure, from shock or massive bleeding; A rise in blood pressure which may cause aneurisms (cerebral hemorrhage).

The cerebral vascular system is fairly consistent, thus the effect on damages in specific arteries has similar effect on cognitive functions across most people.

###### Tumors
Tumors or neoplasms is an abnormal growth of tissue and is has no psychological function. They almost always occur in [[Cells and tissue of the Nervous System#Glial Cells|glial cells]] and other [[Cells and tissue of the Nervous System#White matter|white matter tissues]]. 
Tumors are benigng, when they do not recur after removal and tebnd to remain in the area of their germination. 

###### Degenerative and infectious disorders
Degenerative disorders have been associated with both genetic aberrations and environmental agents. They are often confirmed with MRI Scans. Viruses can also cause progressive neurological disorders. 
![[Screenshot 2025-02-02 at 17.12.08.png]]

###### Traumatic brain injury (TBI)
Common causes are car accidents, falls, contact sports, etc. Damage does not always occur at the site of the trauma because of the reactive forces that arise as the brain moves within and against the skull. A consequence of TBI is edema (swelling) which can cause an increase in intercranial pressure, in turn reducing the perfusion pressure and flow of blood, resulting in [[#**Vascular disorders**|ischema]] and sometimes the emergence of secondary lesions.
Resent data shows that mild TBI (mTBI), or concussions, might lead to chronic neurodegenerative consequences. 
Diffuse axonal transport (DAI) is commin in TBI, as the twisting disrupts the axonal cytoskeleton. Often the main pathology of mTBI is DAI even without focal lesions and subsequent edema. 

###### Epilepsy
Epilepsy is a condition characterized by excessive and abnormally patterned activity in the brain. The cardinal sympton is a seizure, a temporary loss of consciousness. Other disturbances can be of varying extend, from seemingly nothing, to violent shaking. 
Seizure activity can be confirmed by Electroencephalography (EEG).
![[Screenshot 2025-02-02 at 17.35.49.png]]
The frequency and duration of seizures very greatly, from a hundred seizures a day of a minutes length, to the occasional seizure with a duration of hours. The general population has an eplipsy rate of 0.5% but 5% will experience a seizure at some point. 

##### Studying Brain-Behavior Relationships Following Neural Disruption
Researchers study patients with neurological disorders or brain lesions to examine structure–function relationships. [[#Single and Double Dissociations|Double dissociations]] are better evidence than [[#Single and Double Dissociations|single dissociations ]]that damage to a particular brain region may result in a selective deficit of a certain cognitive operation.
Traumatic brain injuries are the most common cause of brain lesions. Even when the injuries are mild, they can have chronic neurodegenerative consequences.

Impairments in a particular cognitive process do not mean that the damaged part of the brain “performs” that process. Brain lesions can disrupt processing in intact brain structures or disrupt brain networks on a larger scale.
