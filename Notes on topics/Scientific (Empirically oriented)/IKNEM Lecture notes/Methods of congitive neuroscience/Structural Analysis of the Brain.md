##### Visualizing the Gross Anatomy of the Brain

###### Computerized tomography (CT og CAT)
CT-scanning is an extension of x-ray scanning, expanding it to the third dimension, by constructing a 3D space from a series of thinly slices 2D images. For reasons of spatial resolution, it has today been mostly overtaken by MRI.

###### Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI or MR)
An MRI creates a powerful magnetic field within the scanner environment. Which in turn aligns a significant proportion of the protons inside the field (in the brain) parallel with the field. A radio wave is then absorbed by the protons, shifting their axes in a predictable manner and exciting them, which releases energy when the pulse is turned off, and they return to their original orientation. The synchronized rebound produces energy signals that detector surrounding the head pick up. 
Standart MRI scans can resolve structures that are much smaller than 1 mm, allowing great spatial resolution.

##### Visualizing the Structural Connectivity of the Brain

###### Diffusion tenser imaging (DTI)
DTI is performed with an MRI scanner, that measures the density and motion of water molecules in the brain. Using the models of brownian motion (fluid difusion) to elminate isotropic difusion, we can locate the water-motion in the axons of the [[Cells and tissue of the Nervous System#White matter|white matter]], and thereby understand the  anatomical connectivity of the brain.![[Screenshot 2025-02-06 at 22.26.26.png|500]]
