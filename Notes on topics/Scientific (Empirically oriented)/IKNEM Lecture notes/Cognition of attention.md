##### Central questions of Visual Attention
- Where and how does processing happen? (Locus of selection, selection, recognition, and categorisation)
- How much is selected? (Capacity and limits)
- Properties of the information processing:
	- Parallel vs Serial
	- Bottom-up vs Top-down
	- Exogen vs Endogen
	- Automatic vs "Conscious"

##### Three Central Aspects of Attention
- Focus, selected attention
- Divided attention
- Sustained attention (Vigilance)


### Focus, selected attention
Classic paradigm: **Visual Search**
- Searching for a target among distractions or noise
	- For example when searching for a friends at the station among the strangers
- In this type of experiments we measure both reaction time and correctness (Speed-accuracy tradeoff)
- The types of disctraction defines the classification of the task
	- **Feature search**: Target is defined by **one** unique property - It is theorized to be a **parallel process** (Pop-out effect)
	- **Conjunction search**: Target is defined by a combination of properties - Theorized to be a **serial process**, slower than the parallel, especially when the number of distractions is large

### Sustained attention
Measured by **Continuous performance tests** (CPTs) long and frustrating.
A way to shorten these tasks, without compromising with the results:
**Sustained attention response task** (SART) - For example: Clap at every number instead of 3. 

The results of a SART on a group of young people diagnozed with ADHD and a control group with same gender and age (**Fixed**: The numbers is in order, **Random**: the numbers is not in order):
