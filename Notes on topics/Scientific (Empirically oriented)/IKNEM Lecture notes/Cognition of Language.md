##### Written language comprehension
Unlike spoken language comprehension, written language is something we have to learn through targeted instruction

**McClelland and Rumelhart model**
![[Pasted image 20250317112420.png|400]]
The McClelland and Rumelhart model of language comprehension, introduced in the 1980s, is a pioneering work in the field of cognitive psychology and computational linguistics. This model emphasizes the role of parallel distributed processing (PDP) in understanding how humans process and comprehend language. It posits that language comprehension is not a linear, rule-based process but rather an emergent property of the interactions between multiple, interconnected units or nodes in a neural network.

The model suggests that these nodes, representing various linguistic features such as phonemes, words, and syntactic structures, activate and inhibit each other in parallel, leading to the recognition and interpretation of language. This approach highlights the importance of context, prior knowledge, and the dynamic nature of language processing. By simulating these processes through computational models, McClelland and Rumelhart demonstrated how complex linguistic behaviors could emerge from simple, interactive components, providing valuable insights into the cognitive mechanisms underlying language comprehension.
