#### Cognitive taxonomi
**Cognitive control** is "the ability the execute goal-oriented processing/behavior when facing distractions or a lack of outside support
- Often though of as something special for humans, as other animals often have no control of delay of gratification for example
It plays a part in a lot of "human" functions such as:
- Reasoning
- Problem-solving
- Planning
- Language (Symbolic)
- "Executive functions"

It depends on active maintenance and flexible updating of goals over time in order to be able to pursue relevant goals.  (**Engle & Kane, 2004**)

"The core is the distinction between controlled and automated processes and behavior"

###### Lesions and damages to the cognitive control
"**Phineas P. Gage** (1823–1860) was an American railroad construction foreman remembered for his improbable  survival of an accident in which a large iron rod was driven completely through his head, destroying much of his brain's left frontal lobe], and for that injury's reported effects on his personality and behavior over the remaining 12 years of his life‍—‌effects sufficiently profound that friends saw him (for a time at least) as "no longer Gage"."
![[Screenshot 2025-03-24 at 11.41.38.png|100]]
**Typical symptoms of frontal lesions:**
- Seems undisturbed on the surface
- Disturbed ability to plan
- Impulsivity problems
- Utilization behavior
	- involves someone grabbing objects in view and starting the 'appropriate' behavior associated with it at an 'inappropriate' time
	- dependent on the stimuli of the environment to initiate action
- A tendency to perseveration
	- when someone “gets stuck” on a topic or an idea
	- People who perseverate often say the same thing or behave in the same way over and over again
- The basic elements of behavior is intact, but there is a **lack of flexibility** to shift action or strategy  
- Dysexecutive syndrome (**Lateral lesions**)
	- Typically encompasses emotional, motivational and behavioural symptoms, as well as cognitive deficits
- Dysinhibition syndrome (**Medial/Ventral lesions**)
	- medically recognized as an orientation towards immediate gratification, leading to impulsive behaviour driven by current thoughts, feelings, and external stimuli, without regard for past learning or consideration for future consequences

#### Testing of control and executive functions

**Stop-Signal task (SST)** - tests the ability to inhibit an already instantiated action (stop reaching for the pan, when realizing it is hot)

The subejct presses a button as fast as possible after the go signal
- In some trials, the stop signal does not occur
- In some trials, the stop signal occurs fast after the go signal
- In same trials, the stop signal occurs late
 ![[Screenshot 2025-04-03 at 08.52.14.png|450]]
![[Screenshot 2025-03-24 at 11.53.43.png]]
Independent variable: SOA (time between go signal and stop signal)
Dependent variable: SSRT (time before stopping after the stop signal)
