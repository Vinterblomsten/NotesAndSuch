##### Stimulus
Variations in air pressure in waves (sound waves) with two features
- Amplitude - Percieved as pitch
- Frequency - Percieved as loudness

##### Physiology
**Outer ear** 
- Pinna - it's form amplifies certain frequencies (especially the middle frequencies relevant for speech)

**Middle ear**
- Tympanic membrane (Eardrum)
- Ossicles (Malleus, incus, stapes) - Again amplification takes place

**Inner ear**
- Cochlea, etc.

**Cochlea**
When preassure is applied to the oval window of the cochlea the liquid inside the cochlea vibrates, which in turn applies motion to the hairs on the basilar membrane. These hairs are located functionally related to the frequency of which they respond.
- Outer hair cells, about 13.000 - Doesn't detect sound, but have a support function -  provides adaptation to different sound levels
- Inner heair cells, about 3.500 - Detects sounds of different frequencies - contribute to 95% of the input to the auditory nerve.

##### Auditory transduction
Displacement of stereocilia mechanically opens ion channels - Inner hair cells depolarizes, opening $Ca^{2+}$ channels at the base - Transmitting glutamate in the synapse
![[Screenshot 2025-02-17 at 10.35.00.png|400]]

##### Primary auditory pathway
Auditory nerve  -> Superior olivary nucleus (Sound localization) -> Inferior colliculus (Auditory guided behavior) -> Medial geniculate nucleus (Thalamus) -> Primary auditory cortex (A1 - temporal lobe)

Mostly **contralateral organization** - The crossing is not 100% though, as ipsilateral signals is transmitted as well
![[Screenshot 2025-02-17 at 10.37.32.png|500]]
##### Cortical organisation
Tonotopic organization - (Sort of like retinotopic) - Neurons at different sites in the cochlea send information to different sites in the primary auditory cortex (A1)

##### Perceptual qualities of sound
Perceptual qualities
- Loudness (related to amplitude)
- Pitch (Related to requency)
- Timbre (more complex quality dependent on multiple signals)

The receptive fields of neurons in the auditory nerve show selectivity to frequency (pitch) and amplitude (loudness)
![[Screenshot 2025-02-17 at 10.43.14.png|300]]

##### Localization of sound
Takes place in the Superior olivary nucleus. There are two mechanism used to determine sound location:
**Inter-aural intensity difference**
- Based on sound attenuation caused by the head.
- Mutual inhibition of input from left and right cochlea
- Usefull for higher frequencies


**Inter-aural time difference** (see figure below)
- Based on **coincidence detecters** locates in the superior olivary nucleus
- Useful for lower frequencies
- Temporal resolution down to 10 microseconds (corresponding to about 1 degree spatial resolution)

##### Cochlear implant
Excessive sound pressure can physically damage the hair cells in the cochlea - This means that no damage has been done to the auditory nerve

A chochlear implant can be used in this case, where a microphone captures the sounds, which can be transmitted by electrical stimulation to the auditory nerve, at which point neuroplasticity makes it possible to recode the auditory processing.
