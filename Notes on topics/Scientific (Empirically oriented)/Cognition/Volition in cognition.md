#### **Libet's paradigm in the study of volition (and free will)**
Participants sat in front of a fast-moving clock and were asked to perform a simple voluntary action (like flexing their wrist or finger) at a moment of their own choosing. While doing so:
- **EEG** recorded brain activity, focusing on the _readiness potential_ (a buildup of motor cortex activity).
- **EMG** measured the exact moment the muscle moved.
- Participants reported the clock position at which they first became aware of their intention to move (_W-time_).
Comparing these showed that the readiness potential began **~500 ms before movement**, while conscious intention appeared only **~200 ms before movement**.

How do we know that the subjects did not subconsciously plan to do the movement at a given placement of the clock?

**ERP in relation to an EEG-measure**
Averaging a bunch if trial measures (to remove noise) with a given start time (often stimulus- or reponse-locked)

The Bereitschaftpotential (readiness potential) - A negativ potential occuring leading up to the instantiation of an action. - It is calculated **response-locked**

**Criticisms of Libet's experiment**
- Narrow definition of conscious intention - Abstract action (move hand), which is missing purpose, planning, stakes - presentation tomorrwo
- Decision to act already made before the experiments
- Incomplete measure of neural action initiation
	- ERP misses crucial brain networks deeper in the brain
	- With response-locked EEG, the RP is always there, but what if the RP is present far more often, and we are just seeing the one present when we choose to repsond
