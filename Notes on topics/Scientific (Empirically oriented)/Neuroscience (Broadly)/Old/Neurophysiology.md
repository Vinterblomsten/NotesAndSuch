Ions that are "dissolved" in the intra- and extracellular fluids: $Ca^{2+}$, $Cl^-$, $Na^+$ and $Ka^+$

Attractive force: Opposite charge (+ & -)

##### EPSP and IPSP
Excitatory Postsynaptic Potentials (EPSPs) and Inhibitory Postsynaptic Potentials (IPSPs) are transient changes in the membrane potential of a neuron that occur in response to neurotransmitter binding at the synapse. EPSPs are depolarizing events, typically caused by the opening of ligand-gated ion channels (e.g., AMPA receptors) that allow Na+ influx, bringing the membrane potential closer to the threshold for firing an action potential. In contrast, IPSPs are hyperpolarizing events, often resulting from the opening of Cl- or K+ channels (e.g., GABA receptors), which make the membrane potential more negative and reduce the likelihood of an action potential. The balance between EPSPs and IPSPs determines whether a neuron will fire, playing a critical role in neural communication and information processing.

##### Mirror neurons
Mirror neurons are a type of brain cell that fires both when an individual performs an action and when they observe the same action performed by another. Discovered in the early 1990s, these neurons are thought to play a crucial role in understanding others' actions, intentions, and emotions, contributing to empathy, learning, and social interactions. Their discovery has significantly influenced research in neuroscience, psychology, and even artificial intelligence. - **Giacomo Rizzolatti**, **Leonardo Fogassi**, and **Vittorio Gallese**
