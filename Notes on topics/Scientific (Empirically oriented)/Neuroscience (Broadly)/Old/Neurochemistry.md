#### Neurochemistry, Psychopharmacology and Methods
Psychopharmacology is the neuropsychological effects of medication on behavior and the function of the central nervous system. 

In the field of cognitive neuroscience, [[Methods to Pertub  Neural Function#Pharmacology| Cognitive Psychopharmacology]] can improve our understanding of normal behavior, and the connections between neuroanatomy and neurochemistry. 

In **Animal models** we use lesion studies, as often as possible with reversible, functional lesions by blocking single neurotransmitter systems or by doing up- or down regulation of transmissions. 

#### Neurotransmitters
There exists a large variety of neurotransmitters (more than 60), so in order to have a clear understanding of what kind of molecules we are categorizing as transmitters, there is a criterion list:
 - The substance exists in presynaptic axon terminals.
 - The presynaptic cell contains appropriate enzymes for synthesizing the substance.
 - The substance is released in significant quantities when action potentials reachthe terminals of the presynaptic cell. 
 - Specific receptors that recognize the released substance exist on the postsynaptic membrane.
 - Experimental application of the substance produces changes in postsynaptic cells.
 - Blocking release of the substance prevents presynaptic activity from affecting the postsynaptic cell.

**The dopaminergic transmission**
Dopamine is removed from the synaptic cleft by
- COMT: Catechol-O-methyl transferase (Breaks down the dopamine)
- DAT: Dopamine transporter (Reuptakes the dopamine)

Looking closer at **COMT**
Catechol-O-methyltransferase (COMT) is an enzyme that degrades
catecholamines dopamine (DA), noradrenaline and adrenaline
- The amount and efficiency of COMT affect the degree to which the specific catecholamines influence the postsynaptic cell
- COMT is particularly important for the regulation of DA in brain regions where the DA transporter (DAT) does not occur in large amounts and therefore cannot regulate the amount of dopamine available – e.g. in prefrontal cortex
- In humans the COMT gene codes for the COMT protein

SNP (Single nucleotide polymorphism) is a common mutation of a single nucleotide, that happens in about 1% of the population - often without effect, but at times it changes phenotype

**$Val^{158}$ Met polymorphism**
- A functional SNP (single-nucleotide polymorphism) in the COMT gene that results in a point mutation (replacing a single base pair: A $\rightarrow$ G by position 158)
- In some the amino acid valine (Val) replaced by methionine (Met)
- The Val-variant of COMT: Quick inactivation of dopamine
- The Met-variant of COMT: Slow inactivation of dopamine
	- more DA in the synapse
	- larger dopaminergic stimulation of the post-synaptic cell
	- Less activity is needed in the area in comparison to Val
	![[Screenshot 2025-02-28 at 11.52.46.png|400]]

The effect on working memory performance in relation to dopamine-inducing-substances:
- A Met/Met would reach a to high level of dopamine, which results in a lower working memory performance, whereas a Val/Val would reach the optimum
