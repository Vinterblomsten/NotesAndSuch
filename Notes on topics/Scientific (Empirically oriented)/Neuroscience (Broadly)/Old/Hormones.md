##### What are hormones?
Hormones are chemicals secreted by endocrine glands into
the blood stream.
These hormones travel through your blood to other cells and
are taken up by receptor molecules in target cells.

##### Types of hormones

- **Amine**: 1-2 amino acids
- **Peptides**: Many amino acids
	- Bind to specific recepters on the membrane, altering the cell function with non-genetic action
- **Steroid**: Able to permeate membranes, and thus acts on intracellular recepters - often entering the nucleus with the recepter stating a protein production with genomic action.
	- Needs a cofactor

#### Control and regulation of hormones

Both "releasing hormones" and "tropic hormones" are leased in the process of releasing actual hormones in the endocrine glands. 
![[Screenshot 2025-03-07 at 10.44.54.png]]
##### Releasing hormones
Hormone with the only purpose to control the release of another hormone.

**In the hypothalamus**
Releasing hormones 
- CORTICOTROPIN-RELEASING HORMONE (CRH)
- THYROTROPIN-RELEASING HORMONE (TRH)
- GONADOTROPIN-RELEASING HORMONE (GnRH)
- GROWTH HORMONE-RELEASING HORMONE (GHRH)

Inhibiting hormones when released from the hypothalamus
- SOMATOSTATIN (inhibits secretion of various hormones, e.g. GH, insulin, etc.)
- DOPAMINE (inhibits prolactine release)

####  The Adrenal Glands
The "anti-stress" glands of the body - Fight or flight mechanism
![[Pasted image 20250307113200.png]]

The **Medulla** secretes the catecholamines (the fast response):
- Adrenaline
- Noradrenialine

This has the effect to prepare the body for action, raising heart rate, raising respiration, slowing gut function, etc.

The **Cortex** secretes adrenocorticois
- Glucocoticoids (e.g. cortisol)
- Mineralocorticoids (aldostreone)
- Sex steriods (adrostenedione - gives male and female apperence)

**Cortisol** is quite important
- It helps maintaining glucose balance in interaction with insuline
- It boosts the immune system (Seen when you get sick after a period of stress - for example during exam days etc.)
![[Pasted image 20250307113641.png|500]]

**Chronic stress** has a some negative symptoms related to elevated cortisol
![[Screenshot 2025-03-07 at 11.38.01.png|600]]
In the brain **chronic elevated cortisol** also has some negative symptoms - it can be toxic to brain cells:
- EFFECT on NEUROGENESIS: It interferes with the production of new brain cells.
- EFFECT on HIPPOCAMPAL VOLUME: Reduction in size.
- EFFECT on COGNITION: Impairment in thinking and memory.


#### Thyroid gland
Secretes the hormones:
- Thyroid hormones (requires iodine supply through diet as the only substance in the body)
	- Thyroxine
	- Triiodothyronine (T-3)
- Calcitonin

**Thyriod hormones** are a bit special, as they bind to specialized receptors inside cells which then
binds to DNA and regulates gene expression - normally done by **steroid hormones**
- General effect on nervous system, maintaining allertness and reflexes.
- Secondary, impairment of cognitive function, and depression
