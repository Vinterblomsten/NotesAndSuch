### General Concept
Our genome is exposed to multiple types of both internal and external stressors, which along with replication errors and inherited predispositions can lead to mutations and major changes in the genome. 

At some point the cells can escape the normal **social controls of the tissue**, existing independently of external signals. This transformation is called **tumorigenesis**.

Cancer can be **defined** as
A genetic disease driven by accumulation of DNA alterations, both genetic and epigenetic, which **disrupts**:
- Normal proliferation
- Differentiation
- Apoptosis
leading to **uncontrolled growth** and the acquisition of **invasive** and metastatic **properties**.

##### Tumors
We distinguish between a **benign** tumor and a **malignant** tumor
- A benign tumor is a **non-cancerous** growth, that does not spread to other parts of the body
- The malignant tumor is a **cancerous** growth that can **breach the basal lamina**, invade nearby tissue and spread
![Screenshot 2026-10-16 at 14.26.57](../z-Figures/Screenshot%202026-10-16%20at%2014.26.57.png)

**Benign tumors** can include
- **Adenoma** - Quite common benign tumors, that forms in the glandular epithelium (E.g. colon)
- Lipoma - Tumor in fat tissue
- Leiomyoma - In smooth muscle tissue
- Fibroma - In fibrous connective tissues
- **Papilloma** - In surface epithelium (E.g. skin)

**Malignant tumors** can include
- **Invasive carcinoma** (80-90% of human cancers) - Epithelial cells
- Sarcoma - Mesenchymal tissues (Bone, muscle, fat)
- **Melanoma** - Melanocytes (Pigment-producing cells in the skin)
- Glioblastoma - Glial-lineage cells
- **Leukemia** - In the blood-forming cells (This is a liquid cancer, meaning that it does not form tumors, but flows freely)

#### Formation of tumors
A **multistep sequence tumor processer**, is the progression from benign tumor to malignant tumor.
- The progression from **Adenoma** to **Carcinoma** (Glandular epithelium)
- The progression from **Papilloma** to **Carcinoma** (Surface epithelium)
- The progressions from **Mole** (skin) to **Melanoma**

But cancers can also occur **de novo** - that means, that they **aren't preceded** by an identifiable benign tumor/lesion.
- Liposarcoma
- Leiomyosarcoma
- Glioblastoma
- **Leukemia**

Many of the cancerous tumors are a result of changes in the **TP53 gene** and the **p53 protein** it encodes.

#### The multistep evolution of a Carcinoma (80-90% of cancers)
The start is **normal epithelium** which evolves into **hyperplasia**, which means that the cells are **well formed**, but there are an **increased number of cells**. 

This can lead to **dysplasia** which includes **abnormal cells** in part of the epithelial thickness (The layers of epithelial cells).

When the **full epithelial thickness becomes abnormal**, cells losing differentiation and with severe nuclear abnormalities, but there are **no invasion** yet we have a **carcinoma in situ**, which when the invasion occur becomes **invasive carcinoma**

This also means that only the **invasive carcinoma** is classfied as a **malignant tumor**. This is where we see the ability to metastasize via lymphatic or blood vessels, and we see the **hallmarks of cancer**.

The full chronology:
Normal epithelium $\rightarrow$ Hyperplasia $\rightarrow$ Dysplasia $\rightarrow$ Carcinoma in situ $\rightarrow$ Invasive carcinoma

**Grey zones** of classification can occur though (E.g. between dysplasia and carcinoma in situ)

### Cancer Genes
There are many different cancer progression mutations, but we classify them in two groups - **oncogenes** and **tumor suppressors**

#### Oncogenes
These genes are mutations of **proto-oncogenes**, which are normal genes that plays an essential role in regulating cell growth, division, and survival. The "**Gas pedal**" of the cell cycle.
- Examples: **RAS**, **EGFR**, **Akt.**

The **oncogenes** are the mutated versions that in some way leads to **gain-of-funcion**. 

A single mutation (in **one chromosome copy**) of the proto-oncogene is sufficient for the oncogene activity to commence. 
![Screenshot 2026-10-16 at 14.54.09](../z-Figures/Screenshot%202026-10-16%20at%2014.54.09.png)
There are multiple possibilities for gein-of-function mutations that can **convert proto-oncogenes  into oncogenes**
- Mutation in coding sequence leading to hyperactivity of protein
- Gene amplification with more transcription and translation
- Mutation in regulatory region
- Fusion with another actively transcribed protein
![Screenshot 2026-10-16 at 14.55.01](../z-Figures/Screenshot%202026-10-16%20at%2014.55.01.png)
#### Tumor Suppressor
Tumor suppressor genes normally acts as the "brake pedal" of the cell cycle, preventing uncontrolled growth by stopping cell division or promoting apoptosis. Some are also involved in DNA repair
- E.g. **PTEN** (Hydrolyzes PIP3 to PIP2), **RasGAP** (Hydrolyses GTP in Ras), **p53**, **Rb**, **BRCA1/2** (homologous recombination factors)

In the case of tumor suppressors, one mutation is not enough for the inactivation of the suppressor - **both copies of the gene** must be affected for the suppression to be inactivated. 
<img src="../z-Figures/Screenshot%202026-10-16%20at%2015.02.40.png" alt="Screenshot 2026-10-16 at 15.02.40" width="645">
This is often what we see in **hereditary cancer predispositions**, where one chromosome gene is inactivated from the start, meaning that only one mutation is needed for the suppressor to be inactivated.

### Hallmarks of Cancer
The hallmarks of cancer are **properties that all cancers share** despite differences in tissue origin and mutations

Originally (and in our curriculum) there were only **six (and genomic instability) hallmarks** - later on 14
![Screenshot 2026-10-16 at 15.11.25](../z-Figures/Screenshot%202026-10-16%20at%2015.11.25.png)

**Sustaining proliferation signalling**
The constant division of cancer cells, that leads to the formation of tumor growths.
- **EGFR amplification** - Leads to hypersensitive growth factor pathways (Oncogene)
- **RAS mutations** - Lead to indefinite activation of the MAPK pathway (Oncogene)

**Evading growth suppressors**
Cancer cells keep growing and dividing despite internal signals 
- **RB1 loss** - G1/S checkpoint control (Tumor suppressor)
- **TP53 mutation** - Loss of p53-dependent transcription of p21 - leads to failure to halt cell cycle after DNA damage (Tumor suppressor)

**Resisting cell death**
Cells that resist apoptosis despite damage (genomic) will only accelerate the mutations. 
- **Bcl2 over-expression** - Blocks mitochondrial apoptosis (Oncogene)
- **TP53 mutation** - Loss of p53-dependent transcription of pro-apoptotic targets (BAX, PUMA, NOXA), so damaged cells are not eliminated (Tumor suppressor)

**Inducing angiogenesis**
The tumors creates their own "Blood supply system" in order to supply nutrients and oxygen to the tumor cells as it grows.
- **VEGF over-expression** - promotes new blood vessel formation (Oncogene)

<img src="../z-Figures/Screenshot%202026-10-16%20at%2015.21.24.png" alt="Screenshot 2026-10-16 at 15.21.24" width="581">

**Enabling replicative immortality**
- **TERT promoter mutation** - Leads to **telomerase reactivation**, meaning that cells can divide (almost) indefinitely, as the telemores are reformed (Oncogene)

**Activating invasion and metastasis**
This is the crucial difference between benign and malignant tumors
- **E-cadherin / CDH1 loss** - Leads to loss of cell-cell adhesions, meaning that cells flow freely (Tumor suppressor)
- **TWIST or SNAIL activation** - Initiates the epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition by repressing E-cadherin, allowing the cells to move freely (Oncogene)

**Genomic instability** 
The "plus one", however maybe the most important, as this promotes much faster mutations and thus accelerates the cancerous development
- **SAC mutations** - Leads to **aneuploidy** - weird chromosome segregation with abnormal numbers of each (Neither clean oncogene nor tumor suppressor - checkpoint dysregulation)
- **BRCA1 / BRCA 2** - Defective homologous recombination repairs, meaning that mutations persist (Tumor suppressor) 
	- This is however an attack point, as we can chemically disrupt other repair mechanisms, such that the cancerous DNA is destroyed, while the normal cells with intact homologous recombination repair systems are relatively intact

The hallmarks of cancer allows us to link the molecular biology of cancer with the clinical relevance, by targeting specific hallmarks in treatments.
