There are two classes of enzyme-coupled receptors
- The first group has enzymatic activity it self, when activated - Our focus
- The second group activates a **second enzyme** when activated
	- Cytokin receptors

![Screenshot 2026-10-07 at 08.18.23](../z-Figures/Screenshot%202026-10-07%20at%2008.18.23.png)
#### Recepter Tyrosine Kinase (RTK)
These receptors express enzymatic activity them selves, **phosphorylating tyrosine**.

They are usually single-pass membrane proteins, that exists as monomers until activation (With exceptions such as the dimerized insulin receptors) 

The two strand will upon ligand binding and dimerization phosphorylate each other, and the enzyme is thus **autophosphorylating**.

<img src="../z-Figures/Screenshot%202026-10-07%20at%2008.23.25.png" alt="Screenshot 2026-10-07 at 08.23.25" width="496">

The RTKs have three important pathways - **PLC**, **MAP Kinase** and **PI 3**.

The **ligands** for RTKs are usually **growth factors** (It is in the exam), but it can also be some other molecules such as **insulin**. These ligands can come from **all the four major signalling mechanisms** (paracrine, endocrine, contact-dependent and neuronal). 

This also means that the function of these receptors activating are usually **growth**, **proliferation** and **survival**. This is why they play an important role in **cancers**
- Many drugs works as inhibitors for RTKs (small-molecules kinase inhibitors)

The **phosphate-bonding sites f**or the signalling proteins are mostly **specific**, meaning that each type of protein can bind to one site on the RTK.
![Screenshot 2026-10-07 at 08.31.43](../z-Figures/Screenshot%202026-10-07%20at%2008.31.43.png)

#### Phospholipase C pathway
One of the pathways begins with the RTKs activating **phospholipase C (PLC)** as we know it from the $G_q$ pathway. 
- The PLC cleaves **PIP2** to membrane bound **DAG** and **IP3**
- IP3 activates **$Ca^{2+}$ channels** in the ER releasing $Ca^{2+}$ to the cytosol
- The DAG and $Ca^{2+}$ activates **PKC**, while the $Ca^{2+}$ activates **calmodulin**

#### MAP Kinase pathway
In another pathway an **adaptor protein** with no enzymatic activity binds to the activated RTK, and to this a **Ras-GEF** binds, from where it fascilitates the **swapping of GDP to GTP** in the **RAS** protein.

Similar to **heteromer trimeric G-proteins**, the **monomeric Ras** proteins are activated by another protein facilitating the GDP to GTP swapping, and they have **GTPase activity**, meaning that they them selves can cleave the GTP to GDP. 
![Screenshot 2026-10-07 at 08.41.24](../z-Figures/Screenshot%202026-10-07%20at%2008.41.24.png)


**Ras** doesn't have kinase activity it self. Instead they activate **MAP Kinase Kinase Kinase** with phosphorylates the **MAP Kinase Kinase**, once again phosphorylating the **MAP Kinase**, which phosphorylates **proteins** and **transcription regulators** especially **encouraging proliferation**, but also growth and differentiation.

<img src="../z-Figures/Screenshot%202026-10-14%20at%2014.03.25.png" alt="Screenshot 2026-10-14 at 14.03.25" width="610">

Ras is a [proto-oncogen](Cancer.html) - a mutation **inhibiting the GTPase** activity of Ras will cause it to **stay active indefinitely**, leading to uninhibited proliferation signals. 
- We can use SDS-page and western blotting to analyze phosphorylation of proteins, by using antibodies that recognize the protein, and the phosphorylated version respectively. 

#### PI 3-Kinase pathway
The RTK also has a pathway, that begins with the activation of **PI 3-kinase**. This phosphorylates inisitol phospholipid (**PIP2**) into **PIP3**. 
- PIP3 recruits both **Akt**, and **PDK1**, which together with **PDK2** phosphorylates and activates **Akt**.

<img src="../z-Figures/Screenshot%202026-10-07%20at%2008.48.23.png" alt="Screenshot 2026-10-07 at 08.48.23" width="589">

There are hundreds of target proteins promoting **growth** and **survival**, but most importantly (for us) is **Bad protein**. 

**Bad** inihbits **Bcl2**, but when it is **phosphorylated** by active Akt, it releases the now **active Bcl2**, which **inihbits the apoptosis** of the cell.
![Screenshot 2026-10-16 at 11.23.34](../z-Figures/Screenshot%202026-10-16%20at%2011.23.34.png)
Another target of Akt is **Tor** (not directly but through pathway) which **inihbits protein degradation** and **stimulates protein synthesis** leading to cell growth. 

#### Cancer and Enzyme-Coupled Receptors 
The pathways of enzyme-coupled receptors play an important role in cancerous cells. 

- **Proto-oncogenes** are defined as genes in which cancer mutations lead to **gain of functions** - when we get an constitutive active protein (always active) or hypersynthesis of proteins. 
- **Tumor Suppressors** are defined by mutations leading to **loss of function** - when proteins are inhibited in some way

**Angiogenesis** is the processes of blood vessel formation. It is necessary for tumor growth in the eye (and elsewhere), in order for the tumors to grow in size, while still retaining the nutritional flow (Energy and oxygen).
- **VEGF** is a signalling molecule in paracrine signalling to endothelia cells, that stimulate growth of blood vessels by activating tyrosine kinase receptors (Enzyme coupled receptors)
