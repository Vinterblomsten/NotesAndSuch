### Types of Blots

##### Northern Blot
Gel electrophorese with RNA - Size marker uses knt.  Doesn't need negativity treatment, as DNA is equally negatively charged (by the phosphors) 

##### Southern Blot
Gel electrophorese with DNA - Size marker uses kbp

##### Western Blot (SDS-page)
Gel electrophorese with protein - Size marker uses kDA. Before the electrophoresis, the proteins are denatured by temperature, and SDS is used (as a buffer) to give all proteins uniform negative charge
In contrast with the SDS-Page only, we use antibodies for specific proteins for the western blot, meaning that we can choose what we want to investigate.

#### Antibody based analysis
- Immunhistochemistry and cytochemistry (Histology - Blok E)
- Western blot (Blok D)
- ELISA - the detection of antigens or antibodies, by coating a plate with antigens or -bodies
- Lateral flow test (Hurtigtest)
- ChIP analysis (Blok C)


The antibodies consists of 2x2 symmetrical domains (Light chains and heavy chains) with two antigen binding sites, that varies dependent on the $V_L$ and the $V_H$ domain. 

<img src="../z-Figures/Screenshot%202026-09-30%20at%2016.46.39.png" alt="Screenshot 2026-09-30 at 16.46.39" width="700">

The affinity is dependent on the amount of interactions between the antibodies and the antigens - the higher the affinity, the higher the specificity

A problem is the **fast mutations** of the antigens, that makes the affinity of the antibodies lower - this means that mutations can cause the test to be worser, but still works somewhat, or not work at all.

The **lateral flow test** work by adding the nose stuff directly to the test. The virus particles will bind to primary antibodies with red particles.
- The capillary effect pulls to complexes across the test, where a **test line** covered with (secondary?) antibodies, which accumulates the complexes, revealing the colored line
- A control line catches only the antibodies, even if not bound to the virus, which means that the line will be coloured even if no virus is present
