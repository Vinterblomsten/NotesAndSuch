#### Sugars
<img src="./z-Figures/Screenshot 2026-09-13 at 11.11.15.png" alt="Screenshot 2026-09-13 at 11.11.15" width="220" />
In **aqueous solutions** these monosaccharides form rings between a **ketone group** and a **hydroxyl group**:
<img src="./z-Figures/Screenshot 2026-09-13 at 11.11.55.png" alt="Screenshot 2026-09-13 at 11.11.55" width="220" />
The rotation of the **tertiary carbons** in these rings determine the final sugar, which have quite different biological roles.
<img src="./z-Figures/Screenshot 2026-09-13 at 11.16.19.png" alt="Screenshot 2026-09-13 at 11.16.19" width="220" />

By **condensation reactions** monosaccharides can come together to form **disaccharides**. This binding can continue both linearly or in branches to form **oligosaccharides** (Shorter chains) or **polysaccharides** (Longer chains).
<img src="./z-Figures/Screenshot 2026-09-13 at 11.17.14.png" alt="Screenshot 2026-09-13 at 11.17.14" width="220" />

#### Fatty acids
These are defined as a hydro-carbon tail with a **carboxyl group** at the end.
<img src="./z-Figures/Pasted image 20260913112026.png" alt="Pasted image 20260913112026" width="220" />

- Without any double bonds, they are **straight** and called **saturated** (E.g. stearic acid)
- With double bonds, they bend (form **kinks**)and are called **unsaturated**. The acid **cannot freely rotate** around the double bonds.

Alone they are often charged in the carboxyl group, but they can form **esters** by linking to other molecules. One common occurrence of this is **triacylglyceroles** <img src="./z-Figures/Screenshot 2026-09-13 at 11.24.17.png" alt="Screenshot 2026-09-13 at 11.24.17" width="220" />
