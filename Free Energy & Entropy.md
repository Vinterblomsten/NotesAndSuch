# Free Energy & Entropy

Tags: #Entropy #Energy 

**Second law of thermodynamics**
"In an isolated system the degree of disorder can only increase"
 or
"Systems will change spontaneously towards those arrangements that have the greatest probability"

#### Free energy
**Gibs Free Energy** is defined as the maximum amount of work that may be performed by a thermodynamically closed system at constant temperature and pressure. 
Almost always used to quantify change instead of absolute values:
$$\Delta G=\Delta H-T\Delta S$$
Where $H$ is the enthalpy or internal energy of the system ($J$), $T$ is the external temperature ($K$) and $S$ is the entropy or order of the system ($J\cdot K^{-1}$).

A reaction is **energetically favorable** if $\Delta G<0$. For energetically unfavorable reactions to occur, a **coupled reaction** with an even more negative free energy change most co-occur.

When a reaction is at equilibrium the free energy change is 0: $\Delta G=0$

#### Standard free energy
Whereas $\Delta G$ is dependent on the concentrations of the reactants in a given reaction, the **standard free energy change**: $\Delta G\degree$, is independent and defined as:

For reaction $X\rightleftharpoons Y$ the free energy change is given:
$$\Delta G=\Delta G \degree + RT \ln\frac{[Y]}{[X]}$$
where $R$ is the gas constant in $J\cdot K^{-1}\cdot mol^{-1}$. 
Thus, $\Delta G\degree=\Delta G$ under ideal conditions with $1 M$ concentration of each reactant, as $\ln\frac{[Y]}{[X]}=0$.

We see that **at equilibrium** $\Delta G=0$, so $\Delta G \degree = -RT\ln K$, where $K$ is the equilibrium constant $K=\frac{[Y]}{[X]}$. 

Enzymes can only catalyze **sequential reactions** with a net sum negative $\Delta G \degree$.
