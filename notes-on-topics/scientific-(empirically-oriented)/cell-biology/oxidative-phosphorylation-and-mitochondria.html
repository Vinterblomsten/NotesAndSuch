Morphology of the Mitochondria

The mitochondria is one of the few double-membrane organels of the cell.

  • The outer membrane is covered by porin proteins making it permeable to molecules up to dalton, as well as having specific transport proteins
  • The inner membrane hosts the electron-transport chain and the ATP synthesis. It is highly convoluted forming cristae (infolds), which increases the surface area.
    • Its very impermeable due to the lipid cardiolipin in the membrane
  • The matrix is the inner compartment with the mitochondrial DNA, and hundreds of enzymes for pyruvate oxidation, fatty acid oxydation, citric acid cycle and much more.
    • This also contains the mDNA
  • The intermembrane space contains proteins for phosphorylating nucletides (using ATP), important for the hydrogen gradient, and some pro-apoptotic factors (proteins for apoptosis)

The mitochondria diffuses freely in some cells, while in others, it forms structures to supply ATP to areas of high energy consumption.

  • In cardiac (heart) muscles, they form layers between the myofibril (contraction proteins)
  • In sperm they surround the flagella (motor protein)
  • In some cells they form elongated tubular networks

Mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA)

The mitochondria have their own mitochondrial DNA. This is 100% from the egg cell (mother), which has about 100.000 mitochondria, while the spermatoze has about 1000 mitochondria, that are marked by ubiquitin and later destroyed. (Maternal arvegang in danish)

  • It has 16,569 nucleotide pairs
  • 37 genes coding exclusively for the electron transport chain (making up for about 4% of the proteins in the mitochondria)

The mitochondrial DNA exists in many copies, with no histones, no introns and a very simple DNA repair system.

  • This means that every mutation in the mitochondrial DNA is expressed in some proteins.
  • It also means, that most of the damaged mtDNA copies are destroyed (because of the damage) and thus cant be passed on

Heteroplasmia is defined by having different types of mtDNA in the same tissue while homoplamsmia is defined as having the same mtDNA in all tissue. This describes both mutations and polymorphias

It is completely random which the mitochondria is given to which cell at division, which means that we can end with some cells with mitochondrial diseases, and some without - mitochondrial segregation. This variability is called variabel expressivity

The mitochindria have more roles in the cell, than just ATP synthesis:

Simply put, the mitochondrial function can be broken down into multiple steps

  • Citric Acid Cycle

    The last two are what we call oxidative phosphorylation
Mitochondrial division (and fusion)

The mitochondria can like whole cells split using fission. This is done using dynamine to divide the membranes (just a membrane vesicles). The mtDNA is stocastically divided, and can be uneven in numbers.

We can have a too large amount of mitochondria in a given cell. These can be fused together using membrane proteins

Redox reactions

The process of transporting protons across the membrane, is possible as a result of the reduction/oxidation reactions. In aqueous environments electron carriers, that are reduced (receives an electron) often receives a from the water to balance the charge. On the other hand an electron carrier which is oxidized (looses an electron) also releases a into the water. The trick is, that the transmembrane molecules receives a proton in the water on one side of the membrane, and releases a proton to the water on the other side:

The sequence of redox-reactions of the ECT proceeds spontaneously as they have a negative . This is a result of the difference in electron affinity. Low electron affinity molecules such as NADH readily passes electrons to molecules with higher electron affinity (the NADH dehydrogenase complex).

NADH is a strong electron donor (Very favorably of electron transfers) and as a result is a very weak electron donor. This is measured experimentally for redox pairs () as a redox potential (in volts). The higher the redox potential, the higher the affinity for electrons - that is redox pairs with low potential, transfer their electrons to redox pairs with high potential.

The difference in redox potential is a direct measure of , and thus the describes how energetically favorable the electron transfer actually is.

If the NADH transferred the electrons directly to the would be so huge, that a ton of energy would be released as heat. By splitting the reaction into multiple transfers and thus pumping many protons across the membrane, we utilize as much as the free energy as possible.

The Respiratory Complexes

Each of the complexes utilizes metals with different electron affinities, which allows a donated electron to skip between embedded metal ions within the complex. When transferring between complexes, the electrons are passed to electron carries that can diffuse freely in the lipid bilayer.

  • The iron-sulfur centers have low affinity and are present in the NADH dehydrogenase complex
  • The iron in a heme group have somewhat higher affinity and are present in both cytochrome c reductase, cytochrome c oxidase and cytochrome c itself.
    The slight difference in structure and amino acid environment determines the electron affinity of the ions, and thus can a "staircase" along the complex be built. The energy from the electron transport are though to bring the complexes into a conformation where affinity goes from high to low, and can be released in the inner membrane side

The ubiquinone is a molecule with a long hydrophobic tail and a carbon ring, that can accept two electron (and protons). This picks up electrons from the NADH dehydrogenase complex, and delivers them to the cytochrome c reductase complex.
It can also accept electrons directly from generated in the citric cycle and fatty acid oxidation

The cytochrome c is another electron carrier, that accepts four electrons from the cytochrome c reductase (hence the name), moves along the membrane in the inner membrane space (not in the lipid bilayer), and transfers them to the cytochrome c oxidase (hence the name).

The cytochrome c oxidase is where the is reduced. It has the highest electron affinity in the chain. An oxygen binding site contains a heme group and a copper atom. Here four electrons (and protons) are transferred to resulting in while four other protons are pumped across the membrane.
Toxins such as cyanide is highly dangerous because it binds tightly to the cytochrome c oxidase halting the electron transport.

The proton gradient does not only facilitate the synthesis of ATP, but also other processes.

  • Carrier proteins transports pyruvate, ADP, into the matrix and ATP out of it. This is agains their concentration gradients, and thus requires energy. This is done through coupled transport (Both antiporters and symporters) with the proton influx.
  • Other antiporters utilize the membrane potential created by the electron transport chain to transport (charged) out of the cell and into the cell.

  • It can have direct functional properties such as in bacteria, where the influx of protons drive the rapid rotation of the flagellum
