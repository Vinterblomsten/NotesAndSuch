Properties and functions
It has some general properties that makes it unique from other cells and tissues

  • Avascular - Meaning that it has no direct blood contact - instead they get nutrients trough the basal lamina from the connective tissues.
  • Polarised - Apical (Fremad) / Basal (Base)
    • Rest on the basal lamina over connective tissues
  • Divides cells inner/outer - Keeps out patogens and antigens and resists as mechanical stress
  • Allows directional secretion and absorption
  • Cell contacts meaning that they are tightly packed with their neighbour
  • They go through rapid cell division, because they are usually in vulnerable areas

Cell contacts

Cell contacts are divided into contacts between cells and contacts between extracellular matrix and cells

The important ones to know:

Cell-Cell contacts

The cell-cell contacts are divided into

  • Occluding - Forms barriers that cant be easily passed
  • Adhering - Gives stability and structure
  • Communicating - Intercellular communications


Tight junctions

Also called zonula occludens, tight junctions TJ are complete belts surrounding the cell, and not just point contacts.

Transmembrane proteins - Claudine and occludine (four-pass transmembrane proteins) forms tight bonds between the cells by homophillic interactions between the extracellular domains of the proteins

  • This keeps the cells tight
  • Inhibits intercellular transport between the cells of aqua soluble molecules

The protein networks connects the TJ to the cytoskeleton

  • Linking proteins (Plague protein) called Zonula Occludens (ZO-1/2/3) contains a mix of domains binding to claudin, occludin, signaling proteins, and actin - which fixes it loosly to the cytoskeleton


Functions of TJ

  • It divedes the plasma membrine into the apical and basal (Basolaterale) domain
  • It inhibits lateral diffusion of membrane proteins
  • It allows polar nutrient endocytosis and exocytosis - transcytosis

There can occur errors int he TJ.

  • Claudin 1 can be defect which allows water to diffuse across the skin, which means that dehydration occurs (mice died miv)
  • Lack of claudin 1 can lead to a lot of chronic inflammatory diseases (atopic dermatatis? check it out)

Adherens junctions

These AJ are responsible for the mechanical cobling of the cytoskeleton of neighbouring cells. This is again complete belts and not point contacts.

  • Transmembrane cadherin
  • The adhesion is dependent

Homophillic interactions between intracellular cadherin domains holds the membranes close. The cadherin in turn binds to plague proteins (Zonula adherens), that binds to actine filament, thus connecting it to the cytoskeleton

  • The binding between the cadherin is dependent on the ions - these are located between the cadherins in the extracellular space

The cadherine super family of more then 180 types

  • Allows differentiation of similar cells, which is important for different tissue types and especially neural tubes

Defects in AJ are also prevalent

  • Defect E-cadherin in the bowels
  • The cells cant form adherens junctions and desmosomer
  • Disorganisted apitel with no integrity
  • This results in an increase of pathogens and inflammatory bowels dieases

Desmosomes

The desmosomes are point contacts instead of complete belts

  • They are very strong (Relative to the other contacts)
  • Common in cells with great mechanical stress (Epidermis and heart muscle cells)
  • Non-classical Cadherins – Desmoglein og desmocollin (Fine to remeber cadherins)
  • Binds to intermediary filaments (Different types dependent on cell type) trough plague proteins

Gap junctions

There is no actual contact at gap junctions, instead it is a gap

  • Use for communications
  • Channels between cell membranes
  • The channels in the gap junction consists of two halves called connexons, each of which consists of 6 connexines
    • Allows the passage of small molecules and ions (<1000da)
    • 1.4 nm in diameter

The permeability of the gap junctions can be regulated by among other signal molecules.

They are very common in tissues using rapid communications, such as in heart muscle cells

  • The gap junctions (electrical synapses) allow sending action potentials across the membrane directly
  • Allows simultaneous contractions
  • Mutations in connexins can lead to heart faliure and heart arrhythmia (irregular heart beat)

Basal membrane and Cell-ECM Contact

The basal membrane at the base of the epethilal cells are divided into the basal lamina at the top, and reticular lamina at the bottom.

the extracellular matrix is a network of

  • Fibrose proteins - Collagens
  • Adhesive glycoproteins - Laminin and fibronectine
  • Proteoglycans - Perlecan, decorin, aggrecan
  • Glycosaminglycanes (GAGs)

It provides mechanical strengths and as a mechanical buffer (pillow) between cells. All cells can create the matrix, but the compostision differs between the cells.

  • Fibroblasts - Connective tissues
  • Osteoblasts - Skeletal ECM
  • Chondroblasts - Cartilgae (Brusk) ECM
  • Epithelial cells - Basal lamina ECM

The basal lamina is a specialized ECM, with

  • Adhesive glycoproteins - Laminin
  • Collagen IV
  • Proteoglycanes

The reticular lamina consists of

  • Collagen VII, which is in contact with the collagen IV of the basal lamina
Adhesive glycoproteins

These are extracellular glycoproteins with multiple domains, that are specialised in binding to each other and other molecules, and thus creates a big network (matrix). The glycostructures are great for mechanical buffers.

Proteoglycanes

Long chains of sulfating polysaccharides attached to a smaller protein

  • There are more than 50 types, with great heterogeneity between sugars
  • They attract water, which creates a gel-like substance
  • They form a matrix with the glycoproteins and collagen
  • This acts as a mechanical buffer (stødpude) while allowing diffusion of nutrients, metabolites and hormones
Collagen

Long proteins, the forms long fibers

  • Creates the robe-like super-helices (triple helices)
  • Collagen IV is characteristic for forming net like structure

The basal lamina is thus formed by the interconnections between the criss-crossing laminin, the collagen super-helices, the proteopglycanes, and some other proteins. It forms the extracellular base of the epithelial cells, between the cells and the connective tissues.

The Cell-ECM contact

  • Intracellular we have keratine, plasma stif

There are two kinds of Cell-ECM contacts

  • Hemidesmosomes using intermediary filaments
  • Focal adhesions using actin

Integrines
Transmembrane proteins that connects the cytoskeleton to the ECM

  • Attach the cytoskeleton to the basal membrane
  • They bind to the laminin if the ECM
Hemidesmosomes

Anchors the epithelial cells to the ECM

  • The binds the keratine (intermediary filament) via the integrin to the adhesive glycoproteins in the ECM
  • It is a very strong contact
Focal adhesions

The focal adhesions are cell-ECM contacts but unlike hemidesmosomes they connect with the actin of the cytoskeleton.

  • They consists of plague proteins binding transmembrane integrins binding to fibronectines
  • They are quite common in fibroblast cells - see Cytoskeleton and Protein Filaments for details

Defects in the integrins can have a lot of consequences depending on the type of integrin
