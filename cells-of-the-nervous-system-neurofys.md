#### Neurons (General)
The neuron consists of the **soma** (also called the **perikaryon** without the nucleus) and one or more filament extruding from the soma, called **neurites**.
- **Dendrites** -  short and branched
- **Axon** - single, long and depending the function of the neuron myelinated
##### Morphology of the Neuron
The nucleus of the some is large and appears light, as a result of a low concentration of **chromatin**, meaning that many of the genes of the neurons are **desprialized** and a large activity of transcription. The perikaryon possess many of the *common* organelles and some specialized
- **Nissi-substance** - collection of RNA-full ribosomes and endoplasmatic reticilum
	- Is negatively charged - makes it stainable
- Smooth ER
- Golgi apparatus
- Mitochondria
- Vesicles
- Cytoskeleton consisting of
	- **Neurotubuli** - Mikrotubuli
	- **Neurofilaments** - Intermediary filaments
	- **Mikrofilaments** - Actin filaments

The **dendrites** are very branched with small extruding parts called **dendric spinae** which forms the synapse together with the axon terminal.

The **axon** begins at the **axon hillock**
- This is followed (or overlaps with) and initial segment, which is the area of action potential beginning
- This area lacks nissi-substance and can thus be identified with nissl stains

The axon can vary vastly in length, but it sustains the same diameter throughout. 
- Up to one meter but can be smaller than a milimeter
- In the end, it splits into **telodendria**, and at the end of each telodrindion there is an **axon terminal** or **bouton terminaux**. 
- Some axons have **boutons de passage** along the whole axon.
- All the terminals of the axon including the boutons de passage creates synapses with dendrites of perikarya of other neurons.

Transport inside the axon takes place along the microtubule with the help of different motor proteins. Transport away from the soma is called **anterograde transport** (Towards the + end) and towards the soma **retrograde transport** (Towards the - end).

Some axons are **myelinated**. This is done by the **oligodendrocytes** in the CNS (It myelinates multiple axons) and **Schwann cells** in the PNS (Only a single axon). The myeline is divided into segments by **nodes of Ranvier**, where the neuron membrane is exposed to the extracellular fluid. This myelination promotes the impulse speed, making it essential for long nerves (for example motor- and sensory neurons).

Neurons can be classified by **morphological classification** which often is a clue about the functional classification too. 
- **Unipolar neurons** - In uterus
- **Pseudo unipolar neurons** - In the sensory ganglia of the cranial nerves and the spiral ganglia of the PNS
- **Bipolar neurons** - In the retina and auditory, balance and olfactory ganglia 
- **Multipolar neurons** - Most of the neurons in the CNS
	- **Anterior/Ventral horn cells** (Motoriske **forhornsceller**) (cant find translation) - In medulla spinalis
	- **Pyramid cells** - In cortex cerebri
	- **Purkinje cells**  - In cerebellum (Very branched dendrite tree)
- **Granule neurons** - Common name for a lot of neurons with **small perikaryon**

Other classifications include 
- Neurotransmitter type
- Sensory / motor
- Inhibitory / Excitatory
- Afferent (Signals towards CNS) / Efferent (Signals away from CNS)


Neurons gather in **groups**, which has different structures and names depending on the location of the neurons:

In the **CNS**
- Nucleus (Somata) - Collection of somas where a lot of connections occur
- Cortex (Somata) - A layered structure of neurons (somas) in the enchephalon
- Tractus (Axon) - Collections of axons forming the white matter when myelinated

In the **PNS**
- Ganglion (Somata)
	- Ventral (Frontal) root ganglia - **Motor ganglia** which is a collection of motor somas
	- Dorsal (Posterior) root ganglia - **Sensory ganglia** which is a collection of sensory somas, which are located outside the spinal chord. There are **no synapses in sensory ganglia**
- Nerve (Axon) - collection of sensory or motor axons - can by myelinated by schwann cells for fast impulses

#### Glia cells
The support cells of the nervous system. In the CNS the four primary types are **astrocytes**, **oligodendrocytes**, **ependymocytes** and **microglia**. Histologically they are more prone to colouring as the chromatin is more condensed.

Some glia cells have the **ability to divide** in contrast to neurons

##### Astrocytes
Many extruding parts it is know for its star shape. 
- **Protoplasmatic** in the CNS (Or in gray matter)
- **Fibrous** in the PNS (Or in white matter)
Forms the mechanical support system and place a role **metabolism of transmitters** (GABA and glutamate). They also play a vital role in forming the [Blood-Brain-Barrier (NeuroFys)](./blood-brain-barrier-neurofys.md).

Astrocytes can signal each other via electrical synapses using ATP and the protein IP3.

##### Oligodendrocytes
Forms the myelin sheets in the CNS.
- Has a soma and multiple parts for **myelinating multiple axons**
- Can be stained by immunehistochemistry

##### Micro glia
- Performs **phagocytosis** of dead tissues and microorganisms
- Presents antigen in immunological reactions

##### Ependymocytes
Also called ependymal cells, they are a form of epithelial cells. They form a coating of the ventricle system and spinal chord, resulting in the barrier between the Blood-CSF barrier and the [CSF-Brain barrier](./brain-ventricles-and-cfs-neurofys.md). 
- Covered in **cilia** they produce movement in the CSF
- They also **produce the CSF** as a port of the **choroid plexus**.
- (They are thought to act as neural stem cells, but lacks evidence, that they wont deplete - a requirement for stem cells)

##### Schwann Cells
Forms the myelin sheets of the nerves in the PNS
- Only surrounds **one axon** in contrast with oligodendrocytes

##### Other cells
- **Satellite cells** - Cells that supports the somas in the ganglions of the PNS - sort of the astrocytes of the PNS
- **Endothelial cells** - Forms the inner wall of the capillaries
- **Fibroblast cells** - Connective tissues in meninges
