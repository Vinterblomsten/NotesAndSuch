Biosynthesis most often refers to multi-step, enzyme processes where chemical substances absorbed as nutrients (or previously converted through biosynthesis) serve as enzyme, with conversion by the living organism either into simpler or more complex products.

The **metabolism**, which is the sum total of all chemical reactions happening in the body is divided into two pathways:
- **The Catabolic Pathway**: Breaking down larger molecules - an energetically favorable reaction, that generally generates energy
- **The Anabolic Pathway**: Biosynthesis of larger molecules from smaller - an energetically unfavorable reaction, that generally uses energy

Often times catabolic and anabolic reactions are coupled together through enzymatic catalysis.

As a rule of thumb - **Condensation reactions** require energy while **hydrolysis reactions** releases energy

#### Activated Carriers
The small organic molecules with one or more **energy-rich covalent bond**, that diffuses quickly, are used to "carry" energy from generation sites to biosynthesis sites. Acts as the currencies of the cell.

In some anabolic reactions, another high energy **phosphoanhydride bond** is broken and a monophosphate is released from the ADP, releasing almost as much energy as the first


**High Energy Electron Transfer - $\text{NADH}$ & $\text{NADPH}$**
<img src="./z-Figures/Screenshot 2026-08-24 at 08.34.59.png" alt="Screenshot 2026-08-24 at 08.34.59" width="220" />

The difference between $\text{NADH}$ and $\text{NADPH}$ means that they bind to different enzymes resulting in them being used for different functions. In the cytosol we see that: 
$$[\text{NADH}]<[\text{NAD}^+]$$
$$[\text{NADPH}]>[\text{NADP}^+]$$
Which means that $\text{NADH}$ is used as a **reducing agent**, as it pushes the reaction towards dropping the high energy electron, while $\text{NADP}^+$ is used as an **oxidizing agent** as, as it pushes to reaction towards gaining a high energy electron.

| Activated Carrier                              | Group carried in the High-Energy Bond |
| ---------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| $\text{ATP}$                                   | Phosphate                             |
| $\text{NADH}$, $\text{NADPH}$, $\text{FADH}_2$ | Electrons & hydrogen                  |
| Acetyl CoA                                     | Acetyl group                          |
| Carboxilated biotin                            | Carboxyl group                        |
| S-adenosyl methionine                          | Methyl group                          |
| Uridine Diphosphate Glucose                    | Glucose                               |

Many of the carriers requires ATP to synthesize in the first place, making it the sort of the grand-mother-carrier
