#### Complications during replication
During the replication there occurs **supercoiling**, where the DNA wraps around it self (like a cable when wrapping it around) in front of the replication, as the helicase unwraps the helix. Thus is solved by **topoisomorase I and II**. These enzymes creates a transient cleavage of the helix (one strand for topoisomerase I and the other for II), which allows the DNA to "uncoil", before putting them back together (better word?)

#### Replication of Telomeres
When reaching the end of the DNA, the lead strand will finish as it should, but the **lagging strand** wil finish with a RNA primer at the end, as there is not previous segment to reach it.

The **telomerase** fixes this. At the end of the chromosome, we find a telomore repeat sequence. The telomerase extends the repeating seqeunce on the **lead strand**, such that an RNA primer can be added on the **lagging strand**...

There are $3.2\times10^9$ nucleotides in the human genome, which mean that a primer of length 16 can bind to one single place in the genome ($4^{16}=4.3\times10^9$). This is the principle we use in PCR.

**Polymerase Chain Reaction (PCR)**
A type of polymerase that can tolerate boiling, is the essens of PCR. 
- Denaturing of the DNA by heat (90 degrees)
- Annealing of of two primer (One for each end of the segment of interest) (70 degrees)
- DNA synthesis (90 degrees)
